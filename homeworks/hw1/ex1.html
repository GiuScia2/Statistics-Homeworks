Public-Key Cryptography

Elliptic-Curve Scalar Multiplication

An interactive exploration of private-key to public-key generation using a toy elliptic curve, followed by a statistical analysis of the resulting points.

01 / INTRODUCTION

Understanding the Problem

Before implementing the algorithm, let's introduce the mathematical concepts behind elliptic-curve scalar multiplication.

The Toy Elliptic Curve

For this experiment, we use the following simplified curve:

y² = x³ + 7 (mod 17)

The exercise uses the base point G = (1, 5) and an example private key k = 6.

Our objective is to compute Q = kG through repeated elliptic-curve point addition.

                        04 / DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS
                    

                    
Discussion and Conclusions


                    

                        Interpreting the experimental results
                        and their cryptographic significance.
                    

                


                


                    
1. Coordinate Distribution


                    

                        Scalar multiplication deterministically
                        maps each valid private key to a distinct
                        public point within the subgroup generated
                        by G.
                    


                    

                        However, the individual coordinates are not
                        necessarily uniformly distributed. In our
                        experiment X takes only four distinct values,
                        while Y takes eight. Uniformity of the complete
                        output points does not imply uniformity of
                        their individual coordinates.
                    


                    
2. Bit Distribution and Entropy


                    

                        The distribution of individual coordinate
                        bits is not perfectly balanced. This is
                        influenced by the small finite field and
                        by the mathematical structure of the curve.
                    


                    

                        Entropy describes uncertainty under the
                        chosen input distribution. It must not be
                        confused with computational difficulty
                        or cryptographic security.
                    


                    
3. Correlation and Avalanche Behaviour


                    

                        Pearson correlation can reveal linear
                        relationships between numerical variables,
                        but low correlation does not imply
                        independence or unpredictability.
                    


                    

                        Changing one bit of a private key may change
                        several bits of the encoded public point.
                        However, the classical avalanche criterion
                        is primarily associated with cryptographic
                        hash functions, not elliptic-curve
                        scalar multiplication.
                    


                    
4. One-Way Functions vs Hash Functions


                    

                        Elliptic-curve scalar multiplication is
                        efficient in the forward direction:
                        given k and G, computing Q = kG is easy.
                        On suitably chosen large curves, recovering
                        k from Q is computationally difficult because
                        of the Elliptic Curve Discrete Logarithm
                        Problem (ECDLP).
                    


                    

                        Cryptographic hash functions instead map
                        inputs of arbitrary length to fixed-length
                        digests. They are designed to provide
                        properties such as preimage resistance,
                        second-preimage resistance and collision
                        resistance, together with strong diffusion.
                    


                    

                        Elliptic-curve scalar multiplication is not
                        a general-purpose cryptographic hash function.
                        It produces curve points and preserves an
                        algebraic group structure.
                    


                    
5. Security Limitations


                    

                        Our toy curve is not cryptographically secure.
                        Its subgroup contains only nine points,
                        including the point at infinity, so recovering
                        a private key by exhaustive search is trivial.
                    


                    

                        Real Ethereum uses secp256k1, a much larger
                        elliptic curve with appropriately chosen
                        cryptographic parameters.
                    


                    

                        Final conclusion: statistical experiments
                        help us understand the structure and
                        behaviour of scalar multiplication,
                        but they do not prove one-wayness or
                        cryptographic security.
