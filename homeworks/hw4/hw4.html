02 / EXERCISE 1

Welford's Recurrence Formulas

Update the mean and sum of squares one observation at a time, without storing previous data.

Derivation

Let μk be the mean after k observations, and let SSk = Σ(xi − μk)². Starting from Knuth's mean update, define δ = xk − μk−1.

Mean updateμₖ = μₖ₋₁ + δ/k
Sum-of-squares updateSSₖ = SSₖ₋₁ + δ(xₖ − μₖ)
Sample variance (k ≥ 2)s²ₖ = SSₖ/(k − 1)

To derive the second recurrence, rewrite the old deviations around the new mean. Their cross term vanishes because Σ(xi − μk−1) = 0. Simplifying the remaining terms gives SSk = SSk−1 + δ(xk − μk). Consequently, for k ≥ 3:

s²ₖ = [(k − 2)s²ₖ₋₁ + δ(xₖ − μₖ)]/(k − 1)

For k = 1 the sample variance is undefined; for k = 2 it is SS₂. The denominator k − 1 provides Bessel's correction.

Three-Line Core

const delta = x - mean;
mean += delta / k;
SS += delta * (x - mean);

Increment k before these three lines. Initialize mean = 0 and SS = 0.

Statistical Interpretation

The sample mean and sample variance are unbiased estimators of μ and σ². Thus, their averages across many hackers tend to be close to 5 and 10. Increasing n reduces the variability of the individual estimates: Var(sample mean) = σ²/n = 10/n, and the variability of the sample variance also decreases. Increasing m makes the aggregated averages and displayed distributions more stable.

Why is this useful? Inferential statistics uses finite samples to estimate unknown population parameters. Welford provides a numerically stable, memory-efficient way to update those estimates as observations arrive, without storing the entire sample.
