Stochastic Penetration Processes

Bernoulli & Rademacher Models

An interactive statistical investigation of penetration scores across multiple systems and attackers, exploring sample trajectories, score distributions, the Law of Large Numbers and the Central Limit Theorem.

01 / INTRODUCTION

Modeling Penetration Attempts

Representing the penetration process through independent and identically distributed random increments.

Stochastic Model

Consider n systems and m independent attackers. Each attacker attempts to penetrate the n systems, receiving a random score X at every step.

The accumulated score after n steps is modeled as a sum of independent and identically distributed increments.

MODEL A

Bernoulli

X ∈ {0, 1}

A successful penetration receives a score of 1, with probability p. Otherwise, the score is 0.

MODEL B

Rademacher

X ∈ {-1, +1}

Positive and negative increments occur with equal probability, producing a symmetric zero-mean model.

04 / LAW OF LARGE NUMBERS

Convergence in Probability

Investigating how the average penetration score approaches its expected value as the number of systems increases.

Law of Large Numbers

According to the Law of Large Numbers, the average of independent and identically distributed increments converges in probability to their expected value.

Sₙ / n → E[X] in probability

We estimate the probability that the average score differs from its expected value by more than ε = 0.15.

P(|Sₙ/n − E[X]| > 0.15)

05 / CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREM

Convergence to the Normal Distribution

Compare the empirical distribution of normalized penetration scores with a standard normal distribution.

Central Limit Theorem

In the Rademacher model, the increments have zero mean and unit variance. As n increases, the normalized total converges in distribution to the standard normal distribution.

Sₙ / √n → N(0,1) in distribution

In the non-degenerate Bernoulli model, standardization also requires subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation.

(Sₙ − np) / √(np(1−p)) → N(0,1)

06 / RESULTS AND CONCLUSIONS

Interpreting the Simulations

Understanding the influence of system size, attacker population and normalization.

Interpretation of the Results

Increasing the Number of Systems

The accumulated score becomes more variable in absolute terms, while the average score concentrates around the expected increment.

Increasing the Number of Attackers

More attackers provide more independent observations. This improves the precision of the empirical distributions and statistics without changing the theoretical distribution of one attacker.

Bernoulli Model

The average score converges to p. The expected total increases linearly with n when p is positive.

Rademacher Model

Positive and negative increments balance in expectation. The average converges to zero and the normalized total approaches the standard normal law.

Current Experiment Summary

Run the simulation to display the final interpretation.

Final Conclusion

The simulations demonstrate that the normalization of a stochastic process determines its asymptotic behavior.

The Law of Large Numbers explains the convergence of the average penetration score, while the Central Limit Theorem characterizes the distribution of properly standardized fluctuations.

Increasing n changes the theoretical behavior of the scores. Increasing m makes the empirical approximation more reliable by providing more trajectories.
