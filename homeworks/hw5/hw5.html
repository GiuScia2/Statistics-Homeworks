Why Pathological Functions Matter

Dirichlet function

1 on rationals, 0 on irrationals. Every interval contains both: lower Riemann sums are 0, upper sums are 1, so it is not Riemann-integrable. The rationals have Lebesgue measure zero, giving a Lebesgue integral of 0.

Thomae's function

1/q at a reduced rational p/q, 0 at irrationals. Discontinuous only at rationals (a measure-zero set); Riemann-integrable with integral 0, also Lebesgue-integrable with integral 0.

Sampling warning: every finite-precision grid point is rational by construction. It is mathematically invalid to infer the measure of the rationals from their frequency on a rational grid. This page deliberately shows that failure rather than hiding it.

04 / EXERCISE 2 · MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND

The Standard Normal CDF

From an integral with no elementary antiderivative to a computable special function.

Φ(x) = (1/√(2π)) ∫−∞x exp(−t²/2) dt

The Gaussian density integrates to 1 over the real line. Symmetry lets us evaluate the CDF using a finite integral from 0 to |x|, rather than approximating −∞ with an arbitrary cutoff.

Φ(x) = ½ [1 + erf(x/√2)]

Liouville's theorem on elementary integration establishes that the Gaussian integrand has no elementary antiderivative. This does not mean the CDF is undefined: the error function erf gives an exact expression using a special function.

erf(z) = (2/√π) ∫0z exp(−t²) dt

Practical software still needs a numerical algorithm to evaluate erf or Φ(x). Our three implementations use composite Simpson integration, a truncated Taylor series for erf, and a fast polynomial approximation of erf.

Conclusions

Exercise 1: for Riemann-integrable functions, both correctly refined approaches target the same integral. The level-set simulation additionally requires accurate measurement of preimages; it fails dramatically on dense countable exceptional sets when naïvely sampled.

Exercise 2: Simpson integration and erf power series trade computation for accuracy, while a compact erf approximation offers a fast result with a small, nonzero approximation error. The error function provides an exact mathematical representation, not a way to avoid numerical evaluation.
