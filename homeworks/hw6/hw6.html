Contingency Tables & Statistical Independence

Marginal and Conditional Frequencies · Pearson's Chi-Square Test

Build a contingency table from paired observations, compare observed and expected frequencies, and investigate whether two categorical variables are statistically associated.

01 / THEORY

From Joint Frequencies to Independence

Two categorical variables X and F describe each observation. A contingency table counts every combination of categories.

PROBABILITY

Independence

X and F are independent if, for every category pair, the joint probability equals the product of the marginal probabilities.

P(X = x, F = f) = P(X = x) P(F = f)

Equivalently, P(F = f | X = x) = P(F = f) for each x with positive probability. Knowing X does not change the distribution of F.

STATISTICAL INFERENCE

Independence in a Population

Sample frequencies estimate population probabilities. Even if the population is independent, a finite sample need not factorize exactly.

H0: X and F are independent.

H1: At least one joint probability differs from the product of its marginals.

Pearson's chi-square test measures the discrepancy between observed counts and counts predicted by H0.

Eij = RiCj/N   |   χ² = Σ (Oij − Eij)²/Eij   |   df = (r − 1)(c − 1)

The p-value is the right-tail probability of a chi-square distribution with df degrees of freedom. A small p-value is evidence against H0, not the probability that H0 is true.

05 / DISCUSSION

Association Is Not Causation

Interpret a statistical result in the context of the data-generating process.

Statistical association: a difference between conditional distributions can provide evidence against independence. A non-significant result is insufficient evidence to reject independence, not proof of it. Sample size affects the test's power.

Causality: an association between authentication method and incidents does not establish that the method causes the difference. User behavior, system exposure, organizational policies and other confounders could explain it. A controlled study or an appropriate causal design would be needed.

Assumptions: observations should be independently sampled, with mutually exclusive category counts and adequate expected frequencies. Repeated events from the same account can violate independence of observations. This demo flags any expected count below 5 as a conservative warning. For sparse 2 × 2 tables consider Fisher's exact test. The chi-square p-value is asymptotic, not exact.
