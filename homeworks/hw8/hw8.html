Correlation & Linear Regression

Pearson's r · Two Regression Lines · Least Squares · Statistical Independence

Load a paired numerical dataset, solve both regression problems, visualize their differences, and verify why the signed geometric mean of the regression coefficients equals Pearson's correlation.

01 / CORE CONCEPTS

Correlation Measures; Regression Predicts

Correlation describes the direction and strength of a linear relationship. Regression provides a best-fitting prediction rule by minimizing squared errors.

PEARSON CORRELATION

Symmetric and unitless

r = Sxy / √(Sxx · Syy)

r ranges from −1 to +1. It is unchanged if X and Y are exchanged and detects linear association, not every type of dependence.

ORDINARY LEAST SQUARES

Two different optimization tasks

Y on X: ŷ = ayx + byxx
X on Y: x̂ = axy + bxyy

The first minimizes vertical squared distances; the second minimizes horizontal squared distances. They are not generally inverse functions.

Solving for the intercept and slope

For paired observations (xᵢ,yᵢ), define Sxx = Σ(xᵢ−x̄)², Syy = Σ(yᵢ−ȳ)², and Sxy = Σ(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ). Minimizing Σ[yᵢ−(a+bxᵢ)]² gives the normal equations Σ(yᵢ−a−bxᵢ)=0 and Σxᵢ(yᵢ−a−bxᵢ)=0.

byx = Sxy/Sxx;   ayx = ȳ − byxx̄
bxy = Sxy/Syy;   axy = x̄ − bxyȳ

The two lines pass through the centroid (x̄,ȳ). Their slopes can have different units, whereas r is dimensionless.

05 / ALGEBRAIC VERIFICATION

Why the Geometric Mean Gives |r|

This is the exact relation required by the homework. The sign matters for negative correlations.

byx · bxy = (Sxy/Sxx)(Sxy/Syy) = Sxy²/(Sxx·Syy) = r²
r = sign(Sxy) · √(byxbxy)
Product of slopes
r², computed independently
Signed geometric mean
Numerical discrepancy

Both slopes always have the same sign (or are zero), so their product is nonnegative. Taking only the positive square root returns |r|, not r when the association is negative.

06 / FORMAL PROOF

Independence Implies Zero Correlation

The implication holds whenever the second moments are finite and both variances are positive, so Pearson correlation is defined.

Assume X and Y are independent. Independence implies factorization of expectations:

E[XY] = E[X]E[Y]

By the definition of covariance,

Cov(X,Y) = E[XY] − E[X]E[Y] = 0.

Since the standard deviations are positive,

ρ(X,Y) = Cov(X,Y)/(σXσY) = 0.   ∎

Why the converse is false

Let X be uniformly distributed on {−3,−2,−1,0,1,2,3}, and define Y = X². By symmetry E[X] = 0 and E[XY] = E[X³] = 0, so Cov(X,Y) = 0. Nevertheless Y is completely determined by X, hence they are dependent: for example P(Y=0 | X=0)=1, but P(Y=0)=1/7.

Try the Nonlinear dependence with r = 0 dataset above. Both computed slopes and r are zero although the points lie exactly on a parabola. A zero Pearson correlation rules out linear association, not nonlinear dependence.

Important distinction: The proof concerns population random variables; sample r can be nonzero even when the generating variables are independent, due to sampling variability. Conversely, zero sample r never proves independence.

07 / DISCUSSION

Interpretation and Limits

Correlation vs regression: correlation is symmetric and dimensionless; regression is directional and minimizes errors in the variable being predicted. With an intercept and one predictor, R² = r² for either of the two simple least-squares models, but the two prediction rules are not generally interchangeable.

Association vs causality: even a strong correlation is not proof of a causal link. Confounding variables, selection effects, time trends and outliers can generate misleading associations. A scatterplot and subject-matter knowledge are essential.

Mathematical conditions: both variables must have positive variation for Pearson's r and both slopes to be defined. An ordinary regression line is not a suitable model for every nonlinear pattern.
